# Topics, tags and evidence attributes

The manual's chapters and reading routes own navigation. Tags are secondary retrieval labels, not a replacement folder hierarchy or a measure of reliability. Keep a small controlled vocabulary.

| Topic group | Chapters | Workflow | Tags |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Candidate records and intake | 2, 3 | B, I | records, parsing |

| LinkedIn sourcing | 11, 12 | C, G | linkedin, search-discovery, preferences |
| Profile and credibility | 13, 14 | G, H | profile-evidence, proof, visibility |
| Sharing and integrations | 15 | B, I | integration, records, sharing |
| Consistency and chronology | 16 | I | consistency, provenance |

Source attributes remain separate: source ID, author/publisher, title, date precision, original URL, evidence type, supporting explanation and applicability conditions. Historical describes age, not reliability. Vendor documentation establishes documented capability, not independent outcome evidence. A dated asset snapshot does not establish a general hiring rule.

Search by the user's decision first. Combine a subject tag with its conditions when useful, such as linkedin + integration + permissions. New tags should answer a recurring retrieval need rather than create a synonym for every phrase. Use the source guide to distinguish the type and scope of supporting evidence.
