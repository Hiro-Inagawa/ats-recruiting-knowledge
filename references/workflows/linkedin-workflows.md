# LinkedIn workflows G–I

Use the [topic map](../topic-map.md) to read the owning explanation before applying a workflow. These workflows create a review record; they do not authorize asset changes or external actions.

## Workflow G: Profile evidence audit

Use when deciding whether a profile communicates relevant work credibly, or when a specific claim appears unsupported. Inputs: the current profile text or authorized view, target role or audience, existing work evidence, available time and visibility constraints. A historical snapshot must be labeled with its date.

1. Identify the evaluation question and important claims.
2. Locate contribution, context and proof for each claim across sections and reachable work examples.
3. Separate a missing explanation from missing experience or inaccessible evidence.
4. Classify each finding as supported, needs clarification, needs proof or needs a current fact.
5. Choose the smallest useful next step under the user's constraints.

Resulting record: claim, section, support, audience access, uncertainty, priority and proposed action. Decision criteria: prioritize material truth or evaluation gaps. Leave stylistic differences alone unless they impede the task. A twenty-minute limit can produce a focused review of one claim. Limitations: this does not measure global ranking, verify unseen work or authorize account edits. Read Chapters 11, 13 and 14 plus relevant local evidence.

### Completed hypothetical record: one claim, twenty minutes

Inputs: a current profile, a target senior product-design role, twenty minutes and an existing permitted portfolio case. These facts are invented to demonstrate the workflow.

| Claim / section | Support and audience access | Assessment / priority | Selected action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| “Led design-system adoption” in Experience | Case describes adoption guidance and engineering collaboration, but does not identify the person's contribution; external case is accessible | Needs clarification; high for this role | Ask what the person actually owned. If confirmed, propose a bounded contribution clarification before authorized drafting. |
| “Improved delivery by 40%” in About | No measurement, baseline or attributable result supplied | Needs proof; high material accuracy risk | Do not repeat the number as verified. Request its basis; propose an authorized removal or qualified replacement if unsupported. |
| “AI exploration” in Featured | Permitted case labels a personal prototype and describes tested failure cases; sign-in may be needed for Featured access | Supported as exploration; lower priority | Keep the prototype boundary and direct portfolio route. Do not upgrade it to production experience. |

Decision: spend the available time on the adoption contribution and unsupported metric. Do not redesign the portfolio or add keyword lists. If the user rejects rewriting, deliver the factual findings and missing fact; no account action follows. The record does not establish that a recruiter opened the evidence or that a change will increase response.

## Workflow H: Truthful role-to-profile mapping

Use when assessing how an existing profile relates to a target role, before authorized drafting. Inputs: current role requirements, accepted personal facts, profile evidence, role level, demonstrated projects and practical constraints.

1. Separate required conditions, preferred experience and ambiguous wording.
2. Map each important requirement to direct evidence, transferable evidence or a real gap.
3. Select accurate labels and explanatory context, preserving actual contribution and accepted titles.
4. Assign a suitable section and an evidence route without duplicating everything.
5. Review privacy, unsupported metrics and prototype/production boundaries.

Resulting record: requirement, fact, evidence, gap, section and recommendation. Decision criteria: wording may make real experience understandable but must not create experience, eligibility or rank. Ask a focused question only when the answer changes the recommendation. Limitations: search normalization and private employer queries are unknown. This workflow does not authorize drafting in the user's name or publication. Draft only when requested, preserving established facts and the user's accepted wording. Read Chapters 1, 7, 12, 13 and 16.

### Completed hypothetical record: direct, transferable and missing evidence

Inputs: a role requiring enterprise workflow design and design-system collaboration, with AI evaluation preferred. Accepted hypothetical facts establish workflow research and engineering collaboration, but no production model-evaluation ownership.

| Requirement | Accepted fact and evidence | Fit / section | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enterprise workflow design | Led research and interaction design for a permissions-heavy operations product; permitted case shows decisions | Direct; Experience and case | Explain the actual workflow problem and responsibility using an accurate role label. |
| Design-system collaboration | Used and extended an existing system with engineers; did not establish the whole system | Transferable to a system-building remit; Experience | Describe the contribution precisely. Ask whether system ownership is mandatory if that changes fit. |
| AI evaluation | Personal prototype includes exploratory failure tests; no production evaluation responsibility | Gap for production ownership; clearly labeled Featured case | Keep exploration visible without claiming the preferred capability. If it becomes mandatory, revise the fit decision. |

Decision: map the real workflow experience and collaboration; retain the production AI gap. A stronger label cannot fill it. If a recruiter confirms AI evaluation is only preferred, keep the opportunity in consideration; if mandatory, discuss eligibility rather than inventing experience. This record recommends evidence placement, not publication or a search ranking formula.

## Workflow I: Cross-asset consistency and record review

Use when profile, résumé, portfolio or application records appear to disagree. Inputs: dated versions, submitted-file identity when relevant, accepted source facts, project chronology and known sharing/integration information.

1. Record the exact consequential claim and its date in each asset.
2. Distinguish compatible emphasis, stale version, unclear scope and factual contradiction.
3. Check chronology, ownership and official versus functional role context.
4. Identify the affected object and audience. Do not assume synchronization.
5. Decide whether no change, clarification, an authorized bounded correction or a missing fact is appropriate.

Resulting record: claim, versions, source fact, classification, audience, decision and correction boundary. Decision criteria: correct material inaccuracies when authorized and preserve a trace of past submissions. Different wording may be appropriate. Limitations: private ATS contents and current account settings require direct evidence. Do not alter assets, delete résumés or send corrections from an assessment request. Read Chapters 2, 10, 15 and 16.

### Completed hypothetical record: chronology before correction

Inputs: a January submitted résumé, an October profile and case, and accepted project facts. The hypothetical project deployed in August; individual design responsibility is documented.

| Claim and versions | Source fact / classification | Audience and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| January résumé: “AI prototype”; October case: “deployed in August” | Deployment occurred in August; chronological difference | Preserve the January submission as history. No contradiction to repair; explain the timeline if asked. |
| Profile: “led product design”; résumé: “sole designer”; case names engineers and PM | Person directed design as the only designer, with cross-functional partners; compatible emphasis | Keep wording if that scope is clear. Do not infer people management or erase collaborators. |
| Profile employment end date differs from résumé | Accepted employment record supports the résumé date; factual contradiction | Identify the live profile field as the correction target and propose a bounded authorized correction. Do not claim previously submitted files update automatically. |

Decision: propose correcting only the established date error; retain compatible scope and historical prototype wording. If the user corrects the deployment fact to “still a prototype,” reclassify the October deployment claim as unsupported and revise the proposed correction. No asset edit, deletion or recruiter message is authorized by the review itself.
