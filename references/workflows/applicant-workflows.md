# Applicant workflows A–F

Use the [topic map](../topic-map.md) to read the owning explanation before applying a workflow. These workflows create a review record; they do not authorize asset changes or external actions.

## Workflow A: Requirements to truthful evidence

Use before preparing or assessing an application or interview. Inputs: current role description, employer instructions, actual background and available résumé/portfolio evidence.

1. Record role identity and distinguish stated essentials, preferences and ambiguities.
2. For each consequential requirement, identify a specific example and your contribution.
3. Mark evidence as direct, transferable, unavailable or contradictory. Do not turn transferable into direct.
4. Decide whether to clarify a requirement, improve explanation, choose another example or reconsider fit.
5. Inspect whether the requested materials can establish the claim without relying on unseen information.

Resulting record: `requirement | source/date | actual experience | evidence location | gap | next decision`.

Decision criteria: truthful evidence that answers the actual role; explicit material gaps; no invented experience, impact or eligibility. Limitations: the public description may not capture internal priorities. Ask which ambiguous requirement changes the decision. Worked output: “Regulated domain: not direct; transferable complex workflow case; clarify whether domain is mandatory.” Read chapters 1 and 7.

## Workflow B: Parsing or record problem

Use when imported fields are wrong, a file seems unreadable or outdated information appears. Inputs: exact submitted file, visible form/profile error, role identity and known versions.

1. Separate upload success, visible file content, local extraction and employer import results.
2. Compare the observed wrong field with the actual document.
3. Check allowed format and readable text; inspect available imported fields.
4. Correct only authorized applicant-side fields or prepare a corrected file for the user's review. Do not access or change employer records.
5. Record what was fixed and what remains unverified.

Resulting record: `file/version | observation | local check | actual import evidence | correction | unresolved cause`.

Decision criteria: repair the demonstrated problem, preserve truthful titles and dates, avoid duplicate applications as a speculative fix. Limitations: local extraction does not test every parser; attached document does not establish successful field import. Worked output: “Role field blank; PDF contains correct selectable title; form corrected; employer review status unknown.” Read chapters 2 and 3.

## Workflow C: Search and filter reasoning

Use when assessing discoverability or a claim that a record was excluded. Inputs: known product, query, filter values, searchable scope and actual background; missing inputs stay missing.

1. Separate search terms, field filters and sorting.
2. Check documented syntax and field scope for the actual product.
3. Map genuine terminology to evidence, including accurate synonyms where useful.
4. Identify explanations a query cannot distinguish: unindexed field, stale value, permission boundary or absent evidence.
5. Choose a truthful clarification or the specific missing query/configuration fact.

Resulting record: `query/filter | known scope | evidence term and context | possible exclusion | next check`.

Decision criteria: clarify real experience; do not prescribe keyword density or assume a universal rank. Limitations: an applicant usually cannot see the employer's query. Worked output: “Accessibility work is real but unnamed; clarify it with project context; search inclusion still unverified.” Read Chapter 4 for ATS questions. For LinkedIn questions, also read Chapter 12 and its local LinkedIn evidence guide; establish the product and field before applying syntax.

## Workflow D: Possible rejection explanations

Use after a rejection or when testing an assertion about its cause. Inputs: exact message, submission answers, timeline, known role conditions and any employer explanation.

1. Write the observation without interpretation.
2. List only materially relevant candidate explanations: known eligibility conflict, documented rule, human assessment or changed role state.
3. For each, distinguish evidence supporting it, evidence contradicting it and the missing discriminating fact.
4. Prefer corrections to demonstrated errors and truthful evidence improvements over speculative algorithm optimization.
5. Update when the employer or user supplies new facts; leave the cause unknown if it cannot be resolved.

Resulting record: `observation | candidate explanation | support | contradiction | missing fact | decision`.

Decision criteria: explanation proportional to evidence, no invented probabilities or blame. Limitations: the true cause may remain inaccessible. Worked output: “Minutes-to-email is known; automated rule not confirmed; inspect actual answers before attributing cause.” Read chapters 5, 8 and 10.

## Workflow E: Status or stalled process

Use when status has not changed or a promised next step was missed. Inputs: exact role, dates, confirmed steps, portal text and agreed communication.

1. Construct a short timeline of observations and promises.
2. Separate portal stage from confirmed employer action.
3. Identify the fact that matters now: active vacancy, next step, expected timing or required applicant action.
4. Prepare a factual inquiry only if requested; sending remains a separate user instruction.
5. Keep other application decisions based on their own evidence and revise the record after a confirmed update.

Resulting record: `last confirmed event | displayed state | missed promise | unknown | next decision`.

Decision criteria: seek an answerable fact, respect the agreed channel, do not invent a follow-up interval or success probability. Limitations: some employers do not communicate a usable explanation. Worked output: “Interview complete; promised Friday update absent; role status and next step need confirmation.” Read chapters 8 and 9.

## Workflow F: Compare mechanisms and assess a claim

Use for ATS-score claims, AI explanations, demo interpretations or product comparisons. Inputs: exact claim, available source passage, date, product and alleged event.

1. Classify the mechanism using the comparison table.
2. Identify input, output, decision owner and configuration conditions.
3. Mark whether evidence is product documentation, employer practice, narrated demonstration, original reasoning or actual event evidence.
4. Compare the claim with the assessed local passage; request missing evidence instead of filling it with assumption.
5. Explain the supported conclusion, useful next decision and boundary of that conclusion.

Resulting record: `claim | mechanism | source/passages | conditions | supported conclusion | missing fact`.

Decision criteria: no universalization, preview-to-release substitution or unsupported causal diagnosis. Limitations: product documentation does not prove adoption or actual use. Worked output: “AI-assisted review is documented; this employer's use and rejection mechanism remain unknown.” Read chapters 6 and 10.
